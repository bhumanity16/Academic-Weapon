Why I built this

I once had just one exam the next week, and I underestimated how much studying it would take. I'd already made plans with my friends, and I ended up having to back out of them. I built ScholarSync so students can see how much work they actually have ahead of them, and plan around it.

Three ways to plan

How it works

  1. Pick a plan and fill in your dates, subjects, or tasks.
  2. The AI builds a schedule you can read right on the page.
  3. Save it to the built-in calendar, download it for any calendar app, or send it to Google Calendar.

AI plans are suggestions. Check dates against your school's official schedule.

How ScholarSync is built

A full-stack web app, from the form you fill in to the calendar it lands on.

  1. YouFill in the form in your browser
  2. Flask serverPython, running as a serverless function on Vercel
  3. Groq APIA large language model writes the plan
  4. Your calendarBuilt-in, a standard .ics file, or optional Google Calendar sync

Language models often get weekdays and time arithmetic wrong. The server works out today's date, the day of the week, days until the exam, and how much time fits before your stop time, then gives those facts to the AI.

Plans that fit a school day

Weekday study starts after 4 PM and stays around 2–3 hours, weekends get more, and Today's plan never schedules past the stop time you choose. If your tasks don't fit, it tells you what got cut.

No database

There's no database. Your calendar, streak, and XP live only in your own browser, and Google Calendar is touched only if you sign in and confirm.

Built to be safe in public

  • Every input is checked on the server
  • Requests are rate-limited
  • Google sign-in uses PKCE and state checks
  • The AI's output is sanitized before it's shown on the page
